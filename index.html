Product Overview & Identity

Dimer diamine is a C36 aliphatic primary diamine made from dimerized C18 unsaturated fatty acids (oleic/linoleic, from vegetable oils or tall oil). It is a UVCB substance: a mixture of branched and cyclic isomers rather than one molecule. The long, branched hydrocarbon backbone gives polymers made from it flexibility, very low moisture uptake, hydrophobicity and low dielectric constant [5][21]. Commercial grades are sold as Priamine™ 1071/1073/1074/1075, now owned by Cargill and certified 100% bio-based (USDA BioPreferred, ISCC PLUS) [8].

Synonyms: Amines, C36-alkylenedi-; C36 dimer diamine; dimer amine; DDA; C36-dimer fatty acid diamine (hydrogenated); (1E,19E)-10,11-dioctylicosa-1,19-diene-1,20-diamine (ECHA representative name) [1][2].

Opportunities

Threats

  • Feedstock inflation (TOFA/CTO)
  • Substitute low-Dk chemistries (fluorinated, siloxane diamines)

Feasibility Summary & Management Recommendation

VERDICT: CONDITIONAL — enter via trading, sourcing and CRO; defer manufacturing

Dimer diamine is a niche (est. US$50–150 M) but strategically rising molecule. Its growth comes from low-dielectric electronics and bio-based performance polymers, and supply has consolidated to essentially one producer. India's current demand is thin and fully imported, so a plant would be premature.
